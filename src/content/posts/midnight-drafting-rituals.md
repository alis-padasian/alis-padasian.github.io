People frequently romanticize the writing life: a sunlit studio, an antique oak desk, the effortless outpouring of brilliant chapters over morning espresso.

My actual reality is substantially less cinematic, substantially colder, and almost entirely nocturnal.

## The Witching Hours (11:00 PM – 3:30 AM)

I do my most atmospheric writing between eleven at night and three in the morning. By that hour, the ambient hum of daytime obligations—email notifications, phone calls, household logistics—has completely dissolved.

The silence at 1:00 AM possesses a physical density. When you are writing a scene about a diver trapped beneath an overturned skiff as the tidal surge begins, you need an environment where a sudden house settling sounds like an iron bolt snapping in half.

## Three Non-Negotiable Rituals

1. **Unlined Grid Notebooks:** I sketch every chapter arc by hand with an extra-fine fountain pen before touching a keyboard. Writing by hand forces a slower rhythm that exposes hollow dialogue and lazy scene transitions before they can hide behind rapid typing.
2. **Lapsang Souchong Tea:** Pine-smoked black tea. It smells like a peat fire on a saltmarsh, and the sensory cue acts as a mental trigger that work has officially begun.
3. **Soundtrack of Low-Frequency Drones:** No lyrics, no recognizable melodies. Usually recordings of hydrophones placed in North Atlantic shipping lanes or wind recordings from abandoned coastal radars.

## The Rule of the Morning Read-Through

The hardest discipline in novel writing is not the drafting; it is the brutal light of 8:00 AM the following morning.

Every night's pages must survive the morning test. If the emotional tension that felt electric at 2:00 AM reads melodramatic or sluggish over breakfast, it gets slashed without mercy. The secret to fast-paced commercial fiction is not putting every clever thought onto the page—it is having the courage to leave only the bones that hold the mystery upright.
